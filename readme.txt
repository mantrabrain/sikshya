== Description ==

Sikshya LMS — WordPress learning management plugin for online courses, quizzes, Stripe/PayPal checkout, certificates, and learner dashboards on your domain.

**Sikshya LMS** is a **WordPress LMS** (learning management system) for educators and creators who want students to enroll, learn, and pay without leaving the site. It installs like other WordPress plugins: you control hosting, data, and branding. The free core lets you launch a real course catalog, sell when you are ready, and upgrade to **Sikshya Pro** when you need automation and advanced add-ons.

### At a glance

* **Sell** — Free or paid online courses with Stripe and PayPal, coupons, and orders via [native checkout](https://mantrabrain.com/plugins/sikshya-lms/features/native-checkout/?utm_source=wporg&utm_medium=readme&utm_campaign=feature_native_checkout) in the free core.
* **Teach** — Visual [course builder](https://mantrabrain.com/plugins/sikshya-lms/features/course-builder/?utm_source=wporg&utm_medium=readme&utm_campaign=feature_course_builder), structured curriculum, [video & text lessons](https://mantrabrain.com/plugins/sikshya-lms/features/video-text-lessons/?utm_source=wporg&utm_medium=readme&utm_campaign=feature_video_text_lessons), [quizzes & auto-grading](https://mantrabrain.com/plugins/sikshya-lms/features/quizzes/?utm_source=wporg&utm_medium=readme&utm_campaign=feature_quizzes), assignments, and [course certificates](https://mantrabrain.com/plugins/sikshya-lms/features/basic-certificates/?utm_source=wporg&utm_medium=readme&utm_campaign=feature_basic_certificates).
* **Support learners** — Dashboards, progress, and role-aware flows for instructors and staff.

### Why choose Sikshya?

* **Creator-first workflow** — A fast admin experience (React-powered shell) so you spend time teaching, not hunting through scattered WordPress screens.
* **Commerce that belongs in the free core** — Paid courses, coupons, orders, and mainstream gateways are part of the baseline story—not an afterthought locked behind “contact sales.”
* **Sensible defaults** — Fewer knobs on day one; advanced automation, marketplace, and reporting unlock with **Sikshya Pro** when you are ready to scale.
* **WordPress-native** — Courses, lessons, quizzes, and questions follow familiar custom post types and capabilities, with REST-oriented services where documented—so agencies and developers can extend predictably.
* **Compatible with your stack** — Works with the WordPress block editor (Gutenberg), Classic Editor, and popular page builders such as Elementor and Divi alongside your existing theme.

### Self-hosted WordPress LMS — curriculum, quizzes, checkout, and certificates

Install Sikshya on **your** WordPress site to keep ownership of course content, learner records, and payments—without renting a separate SaaS LMS just to deliver training.

**In plain English:** you create courses in the WordPress admin; learners open your course pages on the front of your site, track progress in their account, and complete quizzes or assignments you publish.

Use Sikshya for coaching, professional training, customer education, internal onboarding, or the start of a course marketplace—with full control of content, branding, and revenue.

### New to WordPress LMS plugins?

* **No code required** to publish lessons and quizzes—you work inside Sikshya’s admin screens like other WordPress plugins.
* **Your theme** controls fonts and many layout basics; Sikshya adds course templates and learner views so selling and learning stay consistent.
* **Start small:** create one course, one short lesson, and one quiz; invite a test student account before you invite paying customers.
* **Payments are optional:** offer free courses first, then connect Stripe or PayPal when you sell.

== Installation ==

1. Install **Sikshya LMS** from **Plugins → Add New** (search “Sikshya” or “Sikshya LMS”) or upload the `sikshya` folder to `wp-content/plugins/`.
2. Activate the plugin.
3. Open **Sikshya** in the WordPress admin menu and walk through setup: required pages, permalink structure (pretty URLs recommended), basic branding, and email sender settings.
4. Under **Payments**, add **Stripe** and/or **PayPal** using **test** keys first; run a small test purchase before switching to live keys.
5. Create your first course, add at least one lesson and (optionally) a quiz, publish, then open the public course URL in a private browser window to see what learners see.
6. Optional: read the [step-by-step Sikshya documentation](https://sikshya.mantrabrain.com/docs/?utm_source=wporg&utm_medium=readme&utm_campaign=documentation) on Mantrabrain for installation detail and privacy controls.

7. Optional: join the [Sikshya LMS Facebook Community](https://www.facebook.com/groups/sikshyalms/) for tips from other site owners.

**Tip:** If anything looks wrong on the front of your site, temporarily switch to a default WordPress theme (Twenty Twenty-Five, etc.) to tell Sikshya styling apart from theme conflicts.

== Frequently Asked Questions ==

= What is an LMS (in one sentence)? =

A **learning management system** is software that hosts your lessons, tracks who finished what, and often handles enrollment or payment—so you are not emailing PDFs and spreadsheets by hand.

= Is Sikshya LMS free? =

Yes. This plugin ships a full free core for building courses, enrolling learners, running quizzes, and selling with baseline checkout features. Advanced modules and priority support are available with **Sikshya Pro**.

= Do I need coding skills? =

No for day-to-day course building. Developers can still extend Sikshya using WordPress hooks, filters, and documented REST endpoints.

= Does Sikshya work with any WordPress theme? =

It is designed for broad theme compatibility. If layouts clash, test with a default WordPress theme to separate theme CSS from LMS templates.

= Does Sikshya work with the block editor? =

Yes. Use the native **Sikshya** blocks (Courses, Login, Registration) or legacy Shortcode blocks with `[sikshya_courses]`, `[sikshya_login]`, and `[sikshya_registration]` — settings and output match. Sikshya course pages and learner templates work alongside block-based pages and standard WordPress themes.

= Does Sikshya work with page builders like Elementor or Divi? =

Yes. Sikshya’s output is standard WordPress templates and shortcodes, so it composes cleanly with **Elementor**, **Divi**, **Beaver Builder**, **Bricks**, and **Oxygen**. Drop the Sikshya shortcodes (`[sikshya_courses]`, `[sikshya_login]`, `[sikshya_registration]`) into any builder widget that renders shortcodes; the rest of your course / lesson / quiz templates render through Sikshya’s own template layer, which respects your theme’s typography and color tokens.

= Can I migrate from LearnDash, Tutor LMS, or LearnPress to Sikshya? =

Sikshya stores courses, lessons, and quizzes as standard custom post types, which makes structured imports possible with tools such as **WP All Import** or with a small migration script targeting the Sikshya REST endpoints. Migrating learner progress (completion history, scores) typically requires a custom mapping — open a support ticket if you’re planning a large migration and want a template script to start from.

= Is Sikshya mobile-responsive? =

Yes. The course catalog, course pages, learner dashboard, quiz player, and learn-page chrome are designed mobile-first with sticky learn-page headers, tap-friendly touch targets (44×44 minimum), keyboard-accessible quiz controls, and ARIA semantics that work with screen readers. Tested on iOS Safari, Android Chrome, and modern WebView contexts.

= Is Sikshya GDPR-compliant? =

Sikshya stores only the learner data you choose to collect (account, enrollments, progress, orders) on **your** WordPress install — nothing is shipped to third-party servers by Sikshya itself. Personal-data export and erasure hooks for WP’s built-in privacy tools are supported, so subject-access and right-to-erasure requests flow through the standard WP **Tools → Export/Erase Personal Data** screens. Any payment, video, or analytics service **you** configure has its own privacy obligations — see the **Use of third-party services** section above.

= What shortcodes and blocks does Sikshya provide? =

Sikshya registers the shortcodes below. Paste them into any page, post, or widget that runs WordPress shortcodes (Shortcode block, Classic editor, or a theme template that calls `do_shortcode`). Attribute names are lowercase unless noted.

**Quick reference**

* **Sikshya Courses** block (or `[sikshya_courses]`) — Grid or list of published courses (same card UI as the catalog).
* **Sikshya Login** block (or `[sikshya_login]`) — Sign-in form (Sikshya auth handler; errors stay on the same page).
* **Sikshya Registration** block (or `[sikshya_registration]`) — Create a Sikshya student account; optional instructor intent submits a pending teaching application.

In the block editor, open the **Sikshya** block category or search for “Sikshya”. Each block exposes the same settings as the matching shortcode attributes in the sidebar; front-end output is identical to the shortcode.

**`[sikshya_courses]`**

**What it does:** Queries published courses and renders them with the same course card partial used on archives and the catalog.

**Attributes** (all optional except where a default is listed):

* `per_page` — Number of courses per page. Default `9`. Minimum `1`, maximum `50`.
* `columns` — Layout hint. `3` forces a three-column grid; other positive values (up to `6`) adjust the auto grid; `0` or omitted uses the default auto layout.
* `view` — `grid` or `list`. Default `grid`.
* `category` — Filter by **course category** taxonomy slug (not the numeric ID).
* `tag` — Filter by **course tag** taxonomy slug.
* `search` — Free-text search string (same idea as the catalog search).
* `orderby` — `date`, `title`, or `price`. Default `date`.
* `order` — `asc` or `desc`. Default `desc`.
* `pagination` — `1` (show paging) or `0` (single page). Default `1`. When enabled, page links use the query argument **`sikshya_courses_page`** so paging does not clash with the main query.

**Examples**

`[sikshya_courses]`

`[sikshya_courses per_page="12" view="grid" category="web-design" orderby="price" order="asc" pagination="1"]`

`[sikshya_courses view="list" search="wordpress" pagination="0"]`

**`[sikshya_login]`**

**What it does:** Renders an email-or-username + password form that authenticates through Sikshya’s `admin-post` handler (`wp_signon`). Failed logins show a notice on the **same URL** (no redirect to `wp-login.php`). Used on the virtual login page and inside checkout.

**Attributes:**

* `redirect_to` — Absolute or relative URL after **successful** login. Validated with `wp_validate_redirect`. If empty, the handler falls back to the HTTP referer, then the site home URL.

**Examples**

`[sikshya_login]`

`[sikshya_login redirect_to="/my-account/"]`

`[sikshya_login redirect_to="https://example.com/checkout/"]`

**`[sikshya_registration]`**

**What it does:** Renders a registration form (display name optional, email, password). Creates a WordPress user with the **Sikshya student** role, then triggers the same **new-user email notifications WordPress sends after core registration** (`wp_send_new_user_notifications`, admin + user). Intended for checkout (“Create account”) and custom landing pages.

**Attributes:**

* `type` — `student` or `instructor`. Default `student`. **`instructor` does not assign the instructor role:** the account is a student and a **pending instructor application** is recorded (same meta as the account “Apply to teach” flow). An administrator approves applications in the dashboard; only then is the `sikshya_instructor` role added.
* `redirect_to` — Same behavior as `[sikshya_login]` after successful registration.

**Developers:** Filter `sikshya_send_new_user_notifications` (bool, user ID) to disable core emails if you replace them with your own.

**Examples**

`[sikshya_registration]`

`[sikshya_registration type="student"]`

`[sikshya_registration type="instructor" redirect_to="/courses/"]`

= 1.0.15 - 2026-11-01 =
**An AI studio for authoring: draft an outline, write a lesson, write quiz questions — with your own provider key, and nothing created until you have read the draft.**
* **New: Course → AI studio.** Describe what a course should teach and get an outline of chapters and lessons you can rename, reorder and delete before anything exists. Pick a lesson and have its body written. Pick a chapter and have quiz questions written, with explanations learners see after grading. Then press a button to create it — generating and creating are deliberately two steps, so a draft you dislike costs you nothing but a click.
* **Your key, your data.** Bring an API key from Anthropic or OpenAI. Requests go from your own server straight to that provider: no course material passes through MantraBrain, and nothing leaves your site until you have saved a key. A monthly request limit is set to 200 out of the box so a mistake cannot run up a provider bill, and it is checked before each request, so a refused one is free.
* Applying an outline creates one chapter at a time with visible progress. If something fails halfway, the chapters already created are real and the rest are still on screen to retry — it never duplicates what already worked.
* AI authoring is off until you enable the add-on, and inert until a key is saved. Updating from 1.0.14 changes nothing about an existing site.
* Requires Sikshya Pro 1.0.11 for the generation itself (Starter tier and above). The studio screen lives in the free plugin so the add-on has somewhere to appear.
* **Deleting a person left the LMS holding their place.** Nothing in Sikshya noticed a WordPress user being deleted, so their enrolments stayed open, their group seat stayed taken, and their mandatory training stayed assigned to somebody who no longer existed. The seat was the expensive part: an organisation that bought a hundred seats lost one with every employee who left, and the only remedy was for a leader to hunt down the row by hand. Deleting a user now closes their open enrolments, and Sikshya Pro releases the seat and records what happened. Completed enrolments are deliberately left alone — that row is the evidence the person did the training before they left — and nothing is deleted. A site that wants the old behaviour can filter `sikshya_cancel_enrollments_on_user_delete`.
* For integrators: a single new action, `sikshya_user_deprovisioned`, carries the departure with the identity attached (login, email, roles, how many enrolments were closed), because by the time it fires the WordPress user record is gone. It is the hook to use for anything of your own that tracks people, and it is what makes an identity provider deprovisioning through a SCIM plugin do the right thing in the LMS — see `docs/ENTERPRISE-IDENTITY.md`.
* **New: a content bank for material more than one course uses.** Content library → Content bank keeps a library of lessons, quizzes and assignments: save anything already in a course, then drop a copy into any section of any course you can edit. A quiz comes with its questions, answer keys and settings. Inserting makes a **copy**, on purpose — a learner's progress and quiz attempts are recorded against one lesson, so a lesson shared between two courses would show somebody as having finished a course they never took. The cost of that is worth knowing before you rely on it: fixing a typo in the bank does not fix the courses already built from it. Items are private to whoever saved them until shared with the site's other authors, and a shared item stays theirs to rename or remove. Starter tier and above, off until you enable the add-on; `docs/CONTENT-BANK.md` covers what travels with a copy and what is refused — cmi5 lessons are, with a reason.
* **Dialogs now trap the keyboard correctly in Safari.** Two focus bugs were hiding behind the fact that everything had only ever been tested in Chrome. On macOS, Safari's Tab order skips buttons and links unless you have turned on full keyboard access, so Sikshya's dialogs calculated where the last stop was and then never reached it — Tab walked straight out of the dialog and into the page behind it. And because clicking a button does not focus it on macOS, opening a dialog with the mouse and pressing Escape dropped you at the top of the page instead of back on the button you pressed. Both are fixed, and the whole browser suite now runs in Chromium, WebKit and Firefox so this class of bug cannot hide again.
* **Checked on PHP 7.4 through 8.5.** The whole test suite now runs on each of 7.4, 8.1, 8.2, 8.3, 8.4 and 8.5, and the compatibility ruleset checks the code against "7.4 and every later version" instead of 7.4 alone. Sikshya still *requires* only PHP 7.4, so no host is left behind — this is about the other end of the range, where a host that has moved to a current PHP should see no deprecation notices from Sikshya.
* **The course picker's search box now actually searches.** Every admin screen that asks you to choose a course — the AI studio, Enrolments, Discussions, the activity log, the gradebook, grading, Integrations, prerequisites, the course builder, orders and coupons — offered a search box that threw away what you typed and listed the first twenty courses by title instead. On a site with twenty courses or fewer nobody could tell, because that list was already all of them; past that, a course whose title did not sort into the first page simply could not be chosen. Typing now filters, and the box keeps what you typed.
* **A warning when Sikshya Pro is older than Sikshya.** Free updates come from WordPress.org, often automatically, while Pro updates come from your account — so for a while after every release the ordinary state of a paid site is the free plugin running ahead of Pro. In that window, screens for features added by a newer Pro add-on appear and then fail to load, which looks like a bug and is not one. Sikshya now says so plainly, naming both versions and where to update, and the notice can be dismissed. A site with no Pro at all never sees it.
* **New: images and audio in quiz questions.** Any question type can now carry one image or audio clip on its stem, shown between the question and the answers — so you can ask "listen to this and tell us what you hear" or "what is wrong with this diagram", which until now simply could not be asked. Pick a file from your media library, or paste a URL. A library file is served through the same signed, expiring, per-learner link as everything else, so a question image behind a paid course is not handed out by its URL. **A picture cannot be saved without a description**, and that is on purpose: the image *is* the question, so without one a learner using a screen reader has nothing to answer from. Audio is exempt — the player gets a sensible name on its own, and a transcript of a clip you are asking somebody to identify would give the answer away. `docs/QUESTION-MEDIA.md` covers what is not claimed: media on the individual answer options is deliberately not built, there is no video, and imports still bring questions across without their pictures.
* **New: PDF lessons.** Set a lesson's type to **PDF document**, pick a file from your media library (or paste a URL), and learners read it in the lesson itself — ticking it complete, counting towards progress and certificates, like any other step. A file from your media library is **not** served by its uploads URL: it goes through a signed link that expires within the hour and only works for the signed-in learner it was issued to, so a URL shared in a chat does not hand your course material to somebody who did not pay for it. There is always a visible "Open in a new tab" link beside the viewer, because embedded PDF support is uneven on phones and a frame that fails to draw does so silently. "Hide the download link" is offered and labelled honestly: it removes the link, and it does not stop anyone saving the file — the browser's own PDF viewer has a save button, and no web page can take it away. `docs/PDF-LESSONS.md` covers the rest, including what is not claimed: no page count or reading progress, no annotations, and no copy protection.
* **New: points and a leaderboard.** People → Points lets you decide what finishing a lesson, passing a quiz, completing an assignment or a course, or earning a certificate is worth. Learners see their total, their place and their last few awards on their account page, and `[sikshya_leaderboard]` puts the board on any page. **Every amount starts at zero**, so turning this on changes nothing until you choose the numbers. Each achievement pays once — un-ticking and re-ticking a lesson, or retaking a quiz, earns nothing more — and a quiz pays for passing, not for attempting. Because a leaderboard publishes real people's names, it is visible to site staff only until you widen it, learners can hide themselves while still earning, names are shortened to a first name and last initial by default, and deleting a person removes their points. Requires Sikshya Pro 1.0.11 (Growth tier and above); `docs/GAMIFICATION.md` covers the rest, including what is not claimed — no streaks, no spending the points, no per-course boards.
* **New: membership levels.** Sikshya could already sell ongoing access, but only one course at a time — set a course's type to subscription and pick its plan. Now Subscriptions → Membership levels lets you say "Gold is these forty courses" (or these categories, or everything), give it a name, a tagline and selling points, and drop the pricing table on any page with `[sikshya_membership_levels]`. A course page tells visitors which memberships include it, and a member sees "you have this" instead of a Join button. **A membership only ever adds access** — a course in no level behaves exactly as it did before, and deleting every level puts things back exactly as they were. Requires Sikshya Pro 1.0.11 (Growth tier and above, part of the Subscriptions add-on); `docs/MEMBERSHIPS.md` covers how joining works and what is not claimed.
* **New: sell your courses through WooCommerce, free.** Edit a product, pick the courses it gives access to, and buying it enrols the customer — in one course or in a whole bundle. A refund, cancellation or failed payment closes the access **that order** gave them, and nothing else: somebody enrolled by an administrator, or who bought the same course in another order, keeps it. Nothing is deleted either, so if they buy again later the same enrolment reopens and the progress they made is still theirs. Guests are handled: WooCommerce will take money from somebody with no account, so one is created from the billing address and WordPress emails them a link to set a password. And a course sold this way shows the WooCommerce price and add-to-cart button instead of Sikshya's own cart, because a page offering two ways to pay makes half of a shop's reporting wrong. Access is granted on **processing** as well as completed, since a shop selling only courses often never marks a digital order complete. Nothing to turn on, and nothing runs if WooCommerce is not installed. `docs/WOOCOMMERCE.md` covers the rest, including what is *not* wired: WooCommerce Subscriptions renewals, and keeping the two prices in step.
* **New: bring a Tutor LMS or LearnDash library across, free.** Sikshya → Tools → Move from another LMS reads the other plugin's courses, structure, lessons, quizzes with their answer keys, enrollments and completions, and recreates them as its own. It never changes or deletes anything belonging to the other plugin, so you can keep it installed, compare the two and switch back — that is the point of doing it this way. Preview first: a preview counts everything a real run would create and creates none of it. And running it twice is safe; the second run updates what it already brought across instead of making a second catalogue. Large libraries are better moved over WP-CLI (`wp sikshya import --source=tutor`, or `--source=learndash`), where there is no request to time out.
* **LearnDash's three levels become Sikshya's two, sensibly.** A course that uses topics becomes a chapter per lesson — with the lesson's own page kept as the first lesson inside it, so nothing the author wrote is lost — while a flat course stays flat, because twenty chapters of one lesson each is faithful and unreadable. Learners who were *given* access but never opened the course come across as enrolled too; they would otherwise be dropped.
* **A question is only imported if it can be imported honestly.** Six types come across exactly as they graded before. Types with no equivalent are named in the report instead of being turned into the nearest thing, and so are matching and ordering questions — those two Sikshya does support, but the old plugin stores their options already in the correct order, so an imported one would be impossible to get wrong. `docs/MIGRATING-TO-SIKSHYA.md` lists every mapping, and what does not come across: no orders or payments, no certificates or reviews, no LearnDash groups, and nothing at all removed from the old plugin.
* **New: cmi5 courses, imported and launched.** Upload a cmi5 package and Sikshya reads its course structure: every block becomes a chapter and every assignable unit becomes a lesson on the course you chose — which is what cmi5 packages are for, and what a SCORM zip cannot do. Learners press Start, the unit opens, and when it reports back what the package calls "done" the lesson is ticked off their curriculum. The unit talks to Sikshya rather than straight to your Learning Record Store, so your LRS credentials never go anywhere near a learner's browser, and whatever a unit claims about who it is reporting on, Sikshya replaces it with the session it was launched under. What is *not* claimed is in `docs/XAPI.md`: this is not an ADL-certified implementation, blocks are flattened into chapters because Sikshya's curriculum is two levels deep, and the importer refuses a package with a completion rule it does not recognise instead of guessing one.
* **Accessibility: a round of fixes you will notice if you use a keyboard or a screen reader.** The account menu button had no name at all on every account page — a screen reader announced "button" and nothing else. Progress bars on the resume card and the course cards reported a percentage without saying what it measured. Dialogs let the Tab key walk out of them onto the page behind, and dropped your place in the page when they closed, so you had to tab all the way back to the button you had just pressed. Seven form fields had labels that were not attached to anything, which reads as "edit, blank". A report table too wide for its box could not be reached without a trackpad. Saying "click Search" to voice control did nothing, because the search button was named something other than the word on it. All fixed.
* **Readable text.** Several status colours sat just under the contrast the standard asks for — the "Remove" link in the cart, passing quiz scores, payment badges, the secondary text throughout the account pages and the learn shell, and the white text on sale and featured badges, which was the worst of them. Each is now a darker shade of the same colour.
* Lists now show "(no title)" for a draft saved without one, instead of a blank row with an invisible link in it.
* The rich-text editor's toolbar buttons — bold, italic, lists, link, text style — had no names, so the editor could not be operated without sight. They do now.
* **New: report learning to your own Learning Record Store (xAPI).** Enrollments, lesson and course completions, quiz results with their scores, assignment submissions and certificates are sent to any conformant LRS as xAPI 1.0.3 statements — the thing nearly every public-sector and corporate tender asks for alongside SCORM, and that no other WordPress LMS does natively. You choose how much about a learner leaves your site: their email address, a hash of it that still lets an LRS recognise the same person elsewhere, or an account id with no email at all. Sikshya → Integrations shows what has been accepted, what is waiting, and what failed with the LRS's own words. Scale tier, off until you enable it, and nothing is queued until an endpoint and credentials exist — so turning it on early cannot build a backlog that floods your LRS later. `docs/XAPI.md` has the full statement map and, just as plainly, what is not included: this is reporting to an LRS, not cmi5, and Sikshya is not itself an LRS.
* For contributors: WCAG 2.1 AA is enforced by the test suite, over all 43 admin screens and 13 learner-facing pages, plus a source check that catches an unlabelled field even when it is hidden behind a tab. What has *not* been done is stated just as plainly: no screen reader has been used, so the conformance report in `docs/ACCESSIBILITY-CONFORMANCE-REPORT.md` is a draft rather than a VPAT. `docs/ACCESSIBILITY.md` has both lists.

= 1.0.14 - 2026-10-16 =
**Groups, cohorts and compliance training get their screens. Tax works, audio lessons are finished, and the session timeout finally measures what it says.**
* **Groups, cohorts and compliance training had no screens at all.** All three shipped as working APIs with nothing in the admin, so there was no way to sell a seat, run a scheduled intake, or read a compliance report anywhere in WordPress. Each now has a full screen under the Sikshya menu. Cohorts leads with the intake's start date, because drip schedules count from it — everyone in one intake reaches week two on the same day however late they joined — and it warns you when an intake has no date, since an undated intake anchors nothing and ordinary per-learner drip applies instead. Compliance shows "overdue" and "completed late" apart from the headline percentage, because an auditor asking whether training was done *on time* is not answered by a figure that counts a late completion as compliant.
* **"Log out inactive users after (minutes)" did not measure inactivity.** It capped how long a login lasted, so somebody halfway through marking a batch of assignments was signed out mid-task at the two-hour mark whatever they were doing — the one thing the setting's own label promises not to do. A session is now extended while the person is active, so only an idle one runs out. The same setting also ignored "Remember Me": a login you explicitly asked to be remembered expired in two hours rather than the fourteen days WordPress promises, with nothing on screen to explain why you kept being signed out. Remembered logins now keep their full length.
* **Importing the sample data twice gave you two of everything.** Pressing Tools → import sample data again, or running the setup wizard after an earlier attempt, created a second copy of every sample course. An import cut short by a hosting time limit was worse still: retrying duplicated the courses it had already made instead of finishing the ones it had not, leaving you with a half-built course sitting beside a copy of it. Each imported course is now recognised, so a later import skips what is already finished and rebuilds only what was interrupted, and the import asks for more time up front so it usually completes in one pass. Courses you wrote yourself are never touched, even if they share a name with a sample one.
* **Tax was a setting that did nothing.** Settings → Commerce has offered a tax rate and a "prices already include tax" switch, and neither was read anywhere — you could configure 8.25% and the store charged nothing extra. Tax is now applied to the amount after any discount, shown on the checkout quote, and recorded on the order. Both modes work: exclusive tax is added on top, and an inclusive price stays exactly what the customer was shown. A rate of 0, which is the default, changes nothing for existing stores.
* **The Email → Templates link was broken.** Opening it printed two PHP warnings into the page and left you there instead of taking you to Email → Templates, because the redirect ran after the page had already started rendering. It now redirects before any output.
* **Admin list screens were slow on busy sites.** The orders screen issued a database query for every order on the page — fetching its lines, each line's course title and the payer separately — and the quiz-attempts screen did the same for every attempt. On a real dataset those two pages cost 65 and 64 queries; they now cost 9 and 6. Nothing about what they show has changed.
* **Audio lessons could not be created.** The lesson player already handled them, but the type was missing from the picker and the server turned an audio lesson into a text one. You can now pick "Audio lesson", paste a direct link to an .mp3, .m4a, .ogg or .wav file, and learners get a player — with the existing transcript field alongside it for accessibility.
* Requires Sikshya Pro 1.0.10. The compliance screen reports on the audience Pro assigns, and Pro 1.0.10 is the release that stopped capping that audience at 5,000 learners.
